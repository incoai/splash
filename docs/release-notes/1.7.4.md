# Splash 1.7.4

Splash 1.7.4 keeps conversations, in less memory and with far fewer SSD writes, and decodes faster. The cache gives up what costs least to lose, in one order in memory and on the SSD; Inkling-2-Mini's and Flint's sliding-window K/V lives only while something reads it; a request that cannot get memory waits for it; and the persistent SSD cache copies a conversation's restore points as it goes.

## Highlights

Against 1.7.3, on the same Macs and the same conversations:

**1. Conversations keep their cache.** Turns compute 38 to 200 times fewer prompt tokens again, and no turn loses its whole prefix.

| Conversations | Tokens computed again | Whole prefixes lost | Requests served |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two with Flint at the memory wall, 48 GB M5 Pro | 3K against 627K | 0 against 7 | 231 against 202 |
| Two with Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS, 24 GB M6 | 5K against 481K | 0 against 4 | 335 against 180 |
| Three with Inkling-2-Mini UD-Q4_K_M to 262K tokens, M5 Max | 14K against 528K | 0 against 3 | 932 against 641 |

- On the 24 GB M6, 99.1% of prompt tokens came from the cache, against 93.3%.
- **After a crash and a restart, each conversation's next turn found about 99% of its context in the persistent SSD cache** (303K of 306K tokens); 1.7.3 hung during the run and, restarted, found none.
- With an SSD cache that has room, a return to an earlier turn computes at most about 8K tokens again.

**2. More context in the same memory.** Sliding-window K/V is held only while something reads it. Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS serves 168,785 tokens of context on a 24 GB M6, against 160,785, and its two conversations at once reached 164K and 156K tokens, against 93K and 79K. Splash reached into the memory kept for macOS 462 and 267 times in two such runs, against 6,175 and 5,192.

**3. A request writes 2.3 to 4.7 times less to the SSD.**

| Conversations | SSD written a request |
| --- | --- |
| Three with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 48 GB M5 Pro | 28 MiB against 133 |
| Three with Inkling-2-Mini UD-Q4_K_M, M5 Max | 44 MiB against 163 |
| Two with Flint, 48 GB M5 Pro | 22 MiB against 63 |
| Three with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, M5 Max | 33 MiB against 82 |
| Two with Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS, 24 GB M6 | 40 MiB against 93 |

A write budget is now a cap: a spent budget stops every cache write, where 1.7.3 wrote on to 3.3 times its burst. The Flint and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B rows of both tables were measured on an earlier build of this release, whose cache code is this one's.

**4. Faster decode and prefill.** On Inkling-2-Mini on a 16-core M5 Pro, a decode step takes 3 to 6% less GPU time at 1 to 8 rows and 12 to 15% less at 12 to 16, with as many tokens kept a cycle, and long prompts prefill 10 to 16% faster; on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on an M5 Max a decode step takes 5 to 11% less at one to four lanes ([performance](#performance)).


What that gives you: a system prompt and tools that conversations share are stored once and kept on the SSD; under memory pressure a conversation keeps part of its history instead of losing it whole; with an SSD cache, a return to an earlier turn recomputes at most about 8K tokens while the SSD cache takes writes and has room for history, a full one keeping what was used more recently ([sparse history](#cache-behaviour)); and the persistent SSD cache survives restarts and crashes: while it has room and no copy waits, a crash loses at most 2,048 tokens and a turn of each conversation, twice that while a copy is being written ([persistent cache](#ssd-cache)).

Also: a request that cannot get memory waits for it, in arrival order, keeping its prefix; a stop lets running requests finish (`--shutdown-timeout`); tool calls, schemas and capacity errors answer as clients expect; a start no longer measures the GPU, so a Mac plans its kernels the same way every start; `--draft-revision` pins the draft.

## Sliding-window storage

Inkling-2-Mini and Flint mix layers that attend to the whole context with layers that attend to a window of recent tokens ([sliding windows](../internals.md#sliding-windows)).

- **Windowed K/V lives only while something reads it.** A request holds only the window it reads, a cached restore point only the window before it, and what nobody holds is free at once. This replaces 1.7.3's trimming, which took memory to free memory, keeps its fix of [#824](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/824) (an idle conversation gives up its history's window K/V before it loses anything), and computes the same tokens: teacher-forced logits and decode outputs equal those of the storage before.
- **More context in the same memory**: the memory plan holds a request's windowed K/V only for its own window. Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS serves 168,785 tokens of context on a 24 GB M6, against 1.7.3's 160,785; in 12 minutes its two conversations reached 164K and 156K tokens with no turn losing its prefix, where 1.7.3's reached 93K and 79K and four of its turns lost their prefix. Two Flint conversations on a 48 GB M5 Pro, with 127,377 tokens of context against 121,361, both reached the run's end at 104K tokens, where 1.7.3's reached 98K and 87K, with 98.75% of prompt tokens from the cache against 92.87%, no lost prefix against seven, 3K tokens computed again by turns against 627K, and 4.8 GiB written to the SSD against 12.7.
- **Where routed experts stream, a capped memory keeps room for cached states** beside the K/V the context needs (`/status` `memory_plan.budget.cached_states_bytes`).
- **On the SSD a windowed copy has two parts**, in `kv.slots` and `kv-window.slots`, the windowed part only where the block held its window K/V when written. On the final build the storage harness, with Inkling-2-Mini on a 24 GB M6 and on a 48 GB M5 Pro and with Flint, restores every point its reference build restores, recomputes at most 896 tokens more than the reference on a return, keeps 6,112 of 7,133 tokens over a restart (Flint 2,560 of 2,966), and resumes a crash where the reference does. Most of its replies differ from the reference's, as this release's kernels sum in other orders ([performance](#performance)), so those checks are judged on these facts, not byte for byte; the cache as merged, before the kernel changes, passed byte for byte but for one intended return ([known issues](#known-issues)).

## Cache behaviour

- **What the cache keeps.** Each conversation's newest restore point; the state at a prompt's head (its system prompt and tools), which the next conversation sharing them resumes from; a reply's last state where the conversation renders its replies back ([#818](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/818)); and on a persistent cache, the point a restart takes back until a deeper one has landed.
- **A point is history once its conversation has gone on past it.** A side request from the tip (a title, a summary, a recall question), a retry or an edit of the last message does not make the tip history; a prompt's head is history only behind a deeper head.
- **When memory runs short, the cache gives up in one order**, for every claim and for the count it is judged by, whether the engine's limit or the host refuses the pages ([#856](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/856); [memory rules](../internals.md#memory-rules)):
  1. what leaves at no loss: checkpoints no running request recovers from, K/V no cached state restores through, and history the SSD already holds;
  2. the rest of history, sparsely: of a conversation's older points, its first and then one every 8,192 tokens are written to the SSD and the others leave unwritten, which bounds a return to an earlier turn ([highlights](#highlights)). A history write takes room only from what the SSD cache can best spare, never from a point's only copy; at a full cache it is refused. With three conversations to 262K tokens and a 7 GiB SSD cache on a 32-core M5 Max, Inkling-2-Mini UD-Q4_K_M wrote 17.4 GiB over its first 641 requests, as many as 1.7.3 served, against 101.9, and its six returns to an earlier turn recomputed nothing, against about 59K tokens each on 1.7.3; five of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's six stayed within the bound ([known issues](#known-issues));
  3. windowed K/V that only history or the points behind a running request hold;
  4. the windowed K/V of points on the SSD, written there as memory needs its pages;
  5. states and K/V by use, the conversation used longest ago first, in three passes: what the SSD holds or a write keeps, then the checkpoints running requests recover from, then what leaving loses;
  6. what running requests use.
- **Where nothing can be written**, with no SSD cache or a spent write budget, the order is the same: what leaves goes without a copy, and what the SSD holds is still read.
- **What the cache frees for a request is what it counted**, so a request short of memory no longer takes prefixes it does not need. On Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS on a 24 GB M6, after the recall questions and a return at 100K tokens, each conversation's next turn resumed from its newest point on the SSD, with 100,544 of 101,308 and 100,384 of 101,182 tokens cached; 1.7.3's conversations did not reach 100K tokens in the same 12 minutes.
- **A request that cannot get memory waits for it**, within the 30-second resource wait, and fails at once only when a fresh count says it cannot fit even alone (`cached_prefix_pages=N` names what it could not take). A refused restore keeps its prefix and waits, never starting cold, where one refusal made a 262,000-token recall whose prefix was on the SSD compute again for 197 s. Requests that waited are served in the order they arrived, after higher priorities (`/status` `admission.stalled_wait_ms`).

## SSD cache

- **A full SSD cache gives up in one order on both tiers** ([SSD cache](../server.md#ssd-cache)): what no restore point needs; then history and the copies of what memory still holds, least recently used first; then a point's only copy, the conversation used longest ago first. A restore makes the copies of the prefix it resumes the most recently used, and a write takes room only from conversations used before its own, else it is refused. Giving history and those copies up together cut a full cache's writes by 50 to 63% on Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on an earlier build, at the same hit rate. On the final build, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's three conversations to 262K tokens wrote 68% less than on 1.7.3 at the same 99.42% hit rate on a 32-core M5 Max (24.5 GiB against 75.6 over 928 requests), and 85% less on a 48 GB M5 Pro (13.3 GiB against 90.6 over 682 requests), at 99.30% against 99.24%.
- **The persistent cache copies restore points by progress** ([persistent cache](../server.md#persistent-cache)): a point once it is 2,048 tokens deeper than the last point a restart would take back, however quick the turns, and a prompt's head at once. A crash so loses no more than the bound in the [highlights](#highlights). 1.7.3 copied only a point that stayed its conversation's newest for 10 s, so a loop of quick turns copied nothing; a point short of a stride now waits for a clean stop, so a crash after a pause can lose up to 2,048 tokens that 1.7.3 had copied. In a loop of quick turns on Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS, a restart after a crash took back 171 restore points (16.0 GiB), and the next turns found 145K of 145K and 158K of 159K tokens cached; 1.7.3 took back 22 points (6.3 GiB) whose next turns found none.
- **A full persistent cache does not rewrite itself**: a copy ahead takes room only from copies nothing needs and from history, and at a full quota the newer point is refused (`write_behind.refused`) until its next stride.
- **A spent write budget is a cap.** With `--max-cache-disk-writes`, a spent budget stops every cache write and the cache keeps to memory, as with `--max-cache-disk 0`, until the budget refills to an eighth of its burst (45 minutes); 1.7.3 let states in use write on, 3.3 times its burst in the audit's run. Only a request whose K/V spills to the SSD writes past it. With Qwen3.6-35B-A3B outgrowing memory for 16 minutes under `--max-cache-disk-writes 20G` (a 5 GiB burst), the final build wrote 121 MiB past the burst and 1.7.3 778 MiB; the final build computed 282K tokens again against 459K and took 98.98% of prompt tokens from the cache against 98.51%, but gave 95% of first tokens within 13.5 s against 4.6, over 500 requests against 428 and deeper prompts. With a budget spent from the start, both wrote one state (19.6 MiB) and nothing more.
- **What the cache writes is counted**: `/status` `disk.written_by` by class, `kv_rewrites`, `quota_given_up` by kind, and `damage` for persistent copies that failed their checksums or a file cut short.

## Weights

- **An F32 tensor a float kernel reads loads as BF16 where every value is a BF16 value**: routers, gates, the small projections the float kernels run, and GDN alpha and beta, in half the bytes. Decode gives the same outputs bit for bit; a prefill chunk of a size between the two formats' tile thresholds takes the faster tile, within the noise floor (Inkling-2-Mini's Unsloth GGUFs at 449 to 576 rows on 20 GPU cores, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 193 to 256). Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS's decode cycle takes 3.2% less GPU time at one row on a 12-core M6 (1.8% at 4 rows, 0.5% at 16), and UD-Q4_K_M's 0.3 to 1.1% less on a 32-core M5 Max.
- **MLX affine modules in groups of 128 load at any width of whole groups** ([#872](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/872)), as every MLX Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's shared expert needs; GGUF PQ2_0 likewise.
- **Inkling-2-Mini's Q8_0 projector runs its Q8_0 values**, where 1.7.3 rounded ggml-org's to BF16: its image takes 329 MiB against 610, and on a 16-core M5 Pro it encoded 1,024 to 3,072 patches 2 to 6% faster and 256 patches 1 to 3% slower, measured on an earlier build of this release.
- **A DFlash draft's BF16 projections load 4-bit (`af4g64`) under either mode**, as in 1.7.3; a DFlash2 draft whose `layer_types` names a layer other than `sliding_attention` is refused by name.

## Serving

- **A tool call is returned only where it is one** ([tool calls](../api.md#tool-calls)): a call of a declared tool, its name matched whatever its case (Flint writes `Bash` as `bash`) and returned as declared. Anything else is the text the model wrote, in every API, where Inkling-2-Mini's text became a call to a tool named `then`; a call the token limit or a stop sequence cut is left out. On Inkling-2-Mini, a call whose header names no tool takes the name its JSON object gives, where that is a declared tool ([#879](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/879)). `/status` `tool_calls.rejected` counts the calls not returned.
- **A response schema the grammar cannot enforce is refused with 400 before generating** where the request asks for strictness (`strict: true` in Chat Completions and Responses, always in Messages), naming the keyword; otherwise it is best effort, where `not` or a look-ahead pattern gave a 500 after the whole reply.
- **A request that cannot fit even alone is a context overflow**: 400 `context_length_exceeded` (Messages: `prompt is too long`), so agents compact and retry, where it was `capacity_exhausted` or `kv_disk_quota`; its message says what helps.
- **An image request that cannot fit is refused before it takes memory**, where a 4000 x 3000 image pushed every cached conversation out of memory first.
- **The start log and `/status` say how many requests the memory runs at once** (`memory_plan.budget.concurrent_requests`).
- **A stop drains.** The first SIGTERM or SIGINT refuses new and waiting requests with 503 `server_shutdown`, answers `/ready` with 503, and gives running ones `--shutdown-timeout` to finish (5 s by default, at most 15, `0` for the old stop); a second signal stops at once.
- **Messages renders a tool's keys in Chat Completions' order**, so on Qwen models a conversation that moves between the two APIs reuses its cached prefix.
- **The cache's hit rate is reported in tokens** (`/status` `cache.token_hit_rate`), and each request's usage says why its reuse stopped (`reuse_end`).
- **`--draft-revision` pins the draft** to a branch, tag or commit, as `--revision` pins the model ([#868](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/868), [#878](https://github.com/incoai/splash/pull/878)).
- **The start says its precision**: how the weights loaded, the KV format, the cached states' storage, the activations and the int8 paths taken; `/status` reports `identity.cache.cached_states` and `activations`.

## Performance

- **Inkling-2-Mini prefills long prompts 10 to 16% faster on a 16-core M5 Pro**, measured on an earlier build of this release against 1.7.3's attention kernels, both on int8 activations (`--activations auto`): its prompt attention runs in tiles that fill the GPU's matrix units (UD-Q4_K_M: 12,700 tokens after a 99,700-token prefix at 793 tokens a second against 686). M3 and M4 GPUs keep 1.7.3's 16-row tiles and speed (an M4's 2,048-token sliding chunk: 12.59 ms against 12.61).
- **Building a command graph takes less host time** ([#875](https://github.com/incoai/splash/pull/875), most of [#755](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/755)): on an M4, 19% less a dispatch of 3 bindings, 2% less at 28.
- **A MoE step of up to 16 rows reads its experts' rows in place, and on M5 GPUs an 8-row expert pass splits its sums**: on a 32-core M5 Max, decode cycles take 0.7 to 3.6% less GPU time on Inkling-2-Mini at 4 to 16 rows and 1.4 to 2.4% less on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 1 to 16 rows; on a 12-core M6, whose passes keep one partition, 0.8 to 3.3% less on both at every row from 1 to 16.
- **Dense decode projections split their sums where a step leaves GPU cores idle**: an 8-row Inkling-2-Mini decode cycle takes 3.5% less GPU time on a 32-core M5 Max, other step sizes and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B level; 16-core M5 Pros keep their plans at 1 to 8 rows, a 12-core M6 is level at every row, and on an M4 4-bit projections run 4 to 9% faster at 1 to 2 rows in a kernel benchmark. These were measured before M5 and M6 GPUs took steps of 3 rows and more to the matrix tile (below).
- **On M5 and M6 GPUs, two or more decode lanes share the threadgroups the GPU keeps resident** for attention: with four lanes on a 20-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's attention pass takes 25% less GPU time at 8 rows and 10% less at 16, and its layers 6 to 37% less on a 32-core M5 Max in a kernel benchmark. One lane is unchanged from 16 GPU cores up; below 16 cores a lone 8-row lane takes fewer splits (on a 12-core M6, 7 instead of 9, its attention layers 7 to 15% faster). M3 and M4 GPUs keep their plans.
- **Under `--activations auto`, Inkling-2-Mini's 8-bit MLX pack prefills on the int8 tile**: an 8K prompt takes 4.6% less GPU time on a 16-core M5 Pro, its projections 25% less, and its teacher-forced logits move 1.2 to 1.5 times as far as its own noise floor: from the int8 prefill, which Inkling-2's default, `bf16`, leaves out ([Upgrade](#upgrade)), and on 32-core GPUs also from the new dense-projection and attention sums. On mlx-community/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-OptiQ-4bit, whose projections are mostly af8, prefill's int8 activations move teacher-forced logits by a pooled KL of 0.00267, about twice that model's step-shape floor (0.00122-0.00128). The top token agrees at 99.04%, at the floor, and the true tokens' NLL is level (0.7895 -> 0.7891). Two of the five sequences exceed 0.005: chat 0.00606 and long 0.00516. This is int8 activations' known cost, the same order as the shipped 35B MLX 4-bit int8 prefill (KL 2.3e-3); `--activations bf16` opts out.
- **A cycle's draft tree and verify rows are laid out in one threadgroup**: decode cycles 0.3 to 0.4% shorter at 5 to 8 lanes on an M5 Max, with the same tokens.
- **A draft grows no row past a stop token**, nor, for a greedy lane, a stop token's own row: on Inkling-2-Mini with its draft, short replies that end on a stop token decoded 2.1% more tokens a second, within the runs' spread, and long replies as before.
- **The decode kernels sum in one written order, and a cycle runs fewer, fuller dispatches.** Every kernel adds in the order its source writes, a fused kernel as the kernels it replaces. On M5 and M6 GPUs a decode step's dense projections of 3 rows and more run on the GPU's matrix units, in 16-row fragments (M3 and M4 GPUs keep the column tile); a step of 9 to 16 rows reads its experts on 16-row tiles; the small kernels between a layer's passes are fused, a one-lane Inkling-2-Mini cycle with its draft taking 888 dispatches against 1,053; a decode lane of at most 5 rows reads its attention on 2- and 5-row tiles; and the message gates run in groups of at most 4 rows. Against this release's code before these kernels, on a 16-core M5 Pro with Inkling-2-Mini's 8-bit MLX pack past its sliding window (a 1,300-token prompt), a decode step takes 3.0 to 6.2% less GPU time at 1 to 8 rows, 12.1% less at 12 and 15.2% less at 16, and a cycle with its draft 2.2 to 6.1% less at 2 to 8 rows; on a 32-core M5 Max, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q4_K_M's step takes 0.4% less at 1 row, 3.4% at 3 and 2.7% at 8. At equal rows the draft keeps as many tokens a cycle: against the code before, the per-prompt ratio is +0.3 to +5.1% over 96 prompts of about 490 tokens on three Macs and -0.1 to +4.8% over up to 48 of about 3,960 tokens on four, none lower beyond its error. A step of more than 64 rows runs its mixer convolution as a dispatch of its own, and no plan reads a rate measured at a start: the matrix tile's partitions come from a table by GPU family and core count.
- **This release's kernels move outputs about as far as reordered sums do, and further where 8-bit affine weights prefill in int8.** Weights load byte for byte where their formats did not change, but the kernels sum in other orders. Teacher-forced, the kernel changes before the written order moved logits no more than one build moves between two step sizes (a mean KL of 0.0013 to 0.0018 nats) on every model checked but the two with 8-bit affine (af8) weights under int8 activations, whose int8 prefill moves them further (above). The written order alone then moved Inkling-2-Mini's by 0.0022 nats, measured when it was made, and the kernels built on it move them 0.0020 to 0.0023 nats from that order's own logits on a 20-core M5 Pro (1.1 to 1.6 times what the build moves between two step sizes, the top token agreeing at 98.8 to 99.0% against 99.0 to 99.2%), as the same reorderings do when stacked on the code before with nothing else changed (0.0024 nats); Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's move 0.0012 to 0.0014 nats from the code before these kernels, inside its floor. The true tokens' likelihood is level on all.
- **"The final build" in these notes is the build the long tests ran**, before the decode kernels above and Inkling-2's default activations ([Upgrade](#upgrade)): those change kernels, their plans and one family's default, not the cache, the SSD tier or the scheduler.
- **The speed gates against 1.7.3**, outputs and weight images compared, pass on every model, on the final build and int8 activations; the decode kernels above take further time off a decode step, and on its default bf16 activations Inkling-2 gives back the int8 prefill's share of these prefill figures (a 1,300-row prefill of its 8-bit MLX pack takes 3.2% more GPU time on a 20-core M5 Pro; [Upgrade](#upgrade)):
  - Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on a 32-core M5 Max: decode 5.4 to 11.2% less GPU time a step at one to four lanes with the decode kernels above (0.7 to 2.0% before them), prefill 1.0 to 1.4% less.
  - Qwen3.8-27B's MLX 4-bit on a 20-core M5 Pro: decode 0.2 to 0.6% more GPU time a step and prefill 0.3% more, within the gate; its weight images identical.
  - Inkling-2-Mini UD-IQ3_XXS on a 12-core M6: decode 1.7 to 3.4% less a step, and 14.7 and 14.0% less a token at three and four lanes, where the draft's acceptance rose from 0.449 to 0.510; a cold 4K-token prompt 6.9% less and a cached one 7.9% less; 168,785 tokens of context against 160,785.
  - Inkling-2-Mini's release on a 16-core M5 Pro, run with `--weights auto`: decode 0.2 to 2.5% less a step, a cold 4K-token prompt 7.6% less and a cached one 8.8% less, short prefills 1.3 to 3.8% less; its weight images identical.

## Fixes

From the 1.7.3 audit:

- **The audit's cache findings are fixed by the changes above**: windowed models keep their prefixes far past where 1.7.3 dropped them (finding 1), which fixes [#758](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/758) partly, Flint's prefixes no longer leaving the SSD cache for whole-page copies, while long sessions still take turns by design, as a new request may not take the memory kept for the system; a crash of a persistent cache takes back recent points, and a full cache writes far less, every write counted (findings 2 and 3, [#720](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/720); `--max-cache-disk-writes` still has no default); a start that loses its cached prefix says why (finding 14); a spent write budget no longer freezes new restore points while others overrun it (finding 15); and a reply's last state survives the memory limit (finding 16, [#818](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/818)).
- **`/status` `memory_governor.claims.waiting` counts every request waiting for memory** (finding 5), **and a wait logs once**, not a thousand `met past the host` lines a second (finding 6).
- **A start that runs out of GPU memory says `not enough free memory to start`** (finding 9), not Metal's `Insufficient Memory`.
- **Offline, a new installation of `REPO:VARIANT` takes the newest cached snapshot that holds the variant** (finding 11).
- **`splash claude` sets `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_SERVER=0`** (finding 17, [#589](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/589), [#849](https://github.com/incoai/splash/pull/849)).
- **A top-level `enable_thinking` is honoured, a non-boolean or conflicting one is refused with 400, and so is `top_logprobs`** (finding 19).
- **The installer shows its download in bytes, and a stop removes its scratch folder** (finding 20).
- **A probe for a path the server does not serve is a quiet 404** (finding 7), **the unsupported-model error names models as the README lists them** (finding 13), and **every `/metrics` series has its type** (finding 21).
- **A client's deadline is one outcome** (finding 22): `requests.timed_out`, worded `request timed out` in every API.

Others:

- **A model part that is not installed is named**, not reported as a raw filesystem error, and the start is not retried.
- **A lone request no longer fails while a cached page it could take stays in memory**, when the SSD quota was full and a copy in flight (present since 1.7.3, found by randomized tests).
- **A return to a windowed point on the SSD no longer fails when memory compacts during its read**, nor risks a crash when memory lets the point go after its request ended: that read fails, and the point is computed again when next asked (found by review).
- **Splash serves `ddalcu/Inkling-2-Mini-MLX-8bit` as published**, which 1.7.3 refused for the model types its configuration leaves out.
- **A prompt's head under a draft window keeps its state** ([#874](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/874), [#876](https://github.com/incoai/splash/pull/876)).
- **`gguf-projection` passes under Metal's shader validation** ([#807](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/807), [#844](https://github.com/incoai/splash/pull/844)) on M5 Max and M4 GPUs, and on the 20-core M5 Pro #807 names, on a build that differs from the final one on the GPU only in M3 and M4 GPUs' decode attention.

## Upgrade

- **Every installation's record is written again once**, from the files it already has: by the desktop app as it launches, or by a model's first start. It gains what the app offers the model's Weights and Thinking menus by.
- **New options**: `--weights original|auto`, `--shutdown-timeout` and `--draft-revision`; the desktop app has the first two as "Weights" and "Stop grace". The app's Activations and Thinking menus name the picked model's own default, and Thinking offers only the efforts its chat template tells apart; sampling has a field each for temperature, top P and top K, which show the model's own values, and the others under More sampling. `--kv-window-trim` is gone.
- **Inkling-2's default `--activations` is `bf16`**: a start that gives no `--activations` runs an Inkling-2 model on bf16 activations, as int8 ones took about 3% off its GGUFs' prefill and 8% off its MLX 4-bit pack's and nothing off a decode step; `--activations auto` rounds them as 1.7.3 did. Every other model's default stays `auto`; the desktop app's "Activations" starts at "Model's Default".
- **Every persistent SSD cache starts cold once**: a cache's namespace now names each weight's format, so the two `--weights` modes and `--activations bf16` keep caches apart, and 1.7.3's caches, unread, go after 14 days unused.
- **`/status`**: `kv.window` drops `trim`, `full_pages`, `slim_pages`, `slim_bytes` and `trimmed_pages` and gains `window_units`; `memory_plan.budget.kv_window` drops `slim_page_bytes`; the staging buffer is its own account, `state_staging`; `requests.failed` and `cancelled` no longer count deadlines. New fields are listed above and in [status](../api.md#status-readiness-and-metrics). The status schema stays 8; the engine protocol is 17.
- **`release-check` against 1.7.3 needs `EXPECT_IMAGE_CHANGE=1`** for models whose formats changed; the pressure gate's G9 fails a 30 s wait only while nothing ahead of it progresses.

## Known issues

- **Two long conversations that no longer fit in memory together cost the idle one a far resume at each switch**: all of it again without an SSD cache, from its last point written under a spent budget. With Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on a 32-core M5 Max, such a pair took 95.95% of prompt tokens from the cache without an SSD cache (1.7.3: 94.78%), losing 9 prefixes (11) and computing 1,788K tokens again (2,669K); under a spent budget, 95.05% (94.00%), 10 prefixes (11) and 1,770K tokens (1,869K).
- **The persistent cache stays opt-in.** At a full quota a crash loses everything past each conversation's last copied point, possibly everything once memory has let go more than the quota holds; a larger `--max-cache-disk` cures both ([persistent cache](../server.md#persistent-cache)).
- **A return to an earlier turn recomputes from the nearest point the SSD kept**, within [the bound](#highlights) while the SSD cache takes writes and has room for history, and the whole prefix, as in 1.7.3, with no SSD cache or a spent write budget; in a rare shape under memory pressure on windowed models (a reply checkpoint above window parts not yet written), a return can recompute more than the bound, a case 1.7.5 removes. As history leaves memory first, a return may recompute where its point could have stayed (896 tokens and about 1 s in the storage harness, on the final cache rules); in exchange no long return recomputes a whole stretch, as 1.7.3's did (about 59K tokens of a 100K-token conversation, in 1.7.3's runs and the rc's). On a 24 GB Mac the next turn can then compute again from the point of the return, though in the final build's runs on a 24 GB M6 the turns after returns at 100K tokens resumed from their newest points ([cache behaviour](#cache-behaviour)). With three conversations to 262K tokens on a 32-core M5 Max, Inkling-2-Mini UD-Q4_K_M's six returns recomputed nothing, the first token in 0.8 s at the median and 2.3 s at most, against about 59K tokens and 38 s each on 1.7.3; of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's six, five recomputed at most 0.1K tokens and one 60.9K after the full cache had given up copies, where three of 1.7.3's recomputed about 60K.
- **Near the minimum memory, where experts stream, a tight cap may serve one request at a time**, as the start now says.
- **Replies are not reproducible under load**, at temperature 0 or a fixed seed: what runs beside a request and whether its prefix came from the cache change them. Of 2,688 recalls from cached conversations on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, measured on an earlier build of this release, three near ties went wrong that a full prefill got right, as on 1.7.3; with `--cached-states exact`, none. A greedy or seeded request repeats its output when it runs alone, under `--verify-rows all` and with a `cache_salt` of its own ([sampling](../api.md#sampling)).
- **A device memory claim can take several states of one conversation**, and **a conversation's second state can outlive another's only state**: the order goes by use, not by what each conversation keeps.
- **On Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with streamed experts, a decode lane retried after a failed expert-block read can produce a different step**, as on 1.7.3; the cause is under investigation.
- **An interrupted download starts again from zero.**
- **A 40-hex `--revision` pins the model alone**: without `--draft-revision`, its first installation online takes the draft repository's newest commit, which later starts keep ([#868](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/868)).
- **A lower-priority request can wait as long as higher-priority ones keep the engine busy** ([#877](https://github.com/incoai/splash/issues/877)), as in 1.7.3.
- **Some request fields are ignored without a word** (Chat's `functions`, `function_call`, `audio`, `modalities`, `web_search_options`; Messages' `mcp_servers`); on Inkling-2-Mini stop sequences match inside the reasoning; structured output at a high temperature can run to `max_tokens` on recursive schemas.
- **Unsloth's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-IQ3_XXS pushes a 64 GB Mac into a memory warning**, as on 1.7.3: on 64 GB serve the suggested `ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF:Q2_0`, or lower `--max-memory`.
- **M3 and M4 Macs were not tested as servers.** On an M4 the final build passes the Metal suites, all 57 (`metal-backend` 60 of 60, twice under shader validation), and the kernels as merged the 18 kernel suites that need no model. cdiamond's Qwen3.8-27B projector was not verified.
