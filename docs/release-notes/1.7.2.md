# Splash 1.7.2

Splash 1.7.2 serves Inkling-2-Mini with its DFlash2 draft, `incoai/Inkling-2-Mini-DFlash2`, from every one of Unsloth's GGUF quants, from its release's weights and from their MLX conversions, and resumes a conversation's next turn at its reply; and decodes it faster: a layer's mixer gates run as one call that normalizes each row once, a block residual mix runs a threadgroup a query, relative attention runs in one pass a layer with history splits fitted to each GPU's cores, a fused projection runs as one dispatch, a row's keys and values project and convolve as one, the FFN convolution runs in the MoE combine, and a 32-core M5 Max splits wide formats less. It also prefills faster: a prompt chunk's block residual mixes run a threadgroup a row, its message gates run one tiled kernel, a one-split chunk's relative attention writes its rows in one pass, an M5 Pro runs a chunk's routed experts on 64-row tiles, and on M5 and later a GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations.

## Speed

On a 16-core M5 Pro (48 GB), runs of each build in turn: Inkling-2-Mini against the build before the decode and prefill work below, with the same draft weights, and the Qwen models against 1.7.1. Each item under Decode and Prefill gives what its change measured where it was made.

- **Inkling-2-Mini decodes at 85.8 tokens a second** with `unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_M` and its draft, one greedy lane, 512-token answers, over 13 prompts, five golden ones and eight clients' chats, where the build before the decode work decodes 78.7 (+9.0%), and at 87.7 with `--verify-rows all`, against 78.7 (+11.4%). By class, at `auto`: code 104.8 against 94.0, math 106.6 against 94.3, chat and prose 75.4 against 70.7, translation 83.5 against 82.2, a 2,583-token document 80.0 against 66.1; four agent prompts 104.8 against 96.5, a 16K-token context 113.1 against 103.0 and a 64K one 98.5 against 86.7.
- **An Inkling-2-Mini decode cycle**, one lane after a 2,048-token prompt, takes 25.6 ms at one row and 48.3 at eight, where the build before the decode work below took 29.3 and 54.3.
- **Inkling-2-Mini prefill** takes 1,796 ms for 2,583 tokens, 11.43 s for 15,869 and 55.52 s for 63,849, where the build before the decode work takes 1,962 ms, 12.42 s and 60.37 s (-8.5%, -8.0%, -8.0%).
- **`mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-4bit`** runs a cycle in 23.7 ms where UD-Q4_K_M takes 25.6 at one row, and in 44.4 where it takes 48.9 at eight, but its draft is accepted less, 5.98 tokens a cycle against 6.30: MLX quantized its attention to 4 bits. Over 512-token answers a token takes about as long, 7.7 ms against 8.0 after the 2,048-token prompt and 8.1 against 8.0 after a code prompt.
- **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.8-27B** (`UD-Q4_K_M`) decode as fast as 1.7.1 or up to 1% faster, one lane at one and eight rows after a 64-token prompt, their outputs identical to 1.7.1's. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B prefills 512 tokens in 179 ms where 1.7.1 takes 184 (-3.0%), and 4,096 in 1,228 ms where it takes 1,278 (-3.6%), its Q8_0 projections on int8 activations; Qwen3.8-27B prefills as fast as 1.7.1.

## Inkling-2-Mini

- **A new family**: Inkling-2-Mini (`thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini`) is a decoder of 24 weight layers over a MoE of 128 experts, eight a token, whose layers 6 to 17 run three times a token, 48 layer applications, each with its own KV and convolution state. Its attention has no RoPE: a relative bias, a sink, an output gate and a window of 1,024 keys on 20 of its 24 layers. Short convolutions follow its keys, its values and every mixer's and FFN's output, a block residual mixes the inputs of layers 0, 6, 12 and 18 into every mixer, FFN and the head, and its logits are softcapped. It is served up to 1,010,000 tokens, below its own window of 1,048,576, in its own chat format, its reasoning and tool calls included, with the sampling its release states: temperature 1.0 over every token.
- **Its DFlash2 draft**, `incoai/Inkling-2-Mini-DFlash2`, installs with it, as every family's draft does. It reads the rows applications 5, 23 and 41 read and the final norm's, and finishes its candidates' logits as its target's head does.
- **A conversation's next turn resumes at its reply's last page**, as the Qwen families' do: its decode captures a reply's state at each page, its convolution state folded there, so a next turn whose prompt renders the reply back prefills only what follows it. In a 27-turn agent session of real tool calls on a 16-core M5 Pro, the turns reused 96.8% of their replies' tokens and prefilled 29K of the 493K prompt tokens they sent; a turn after a 1,663-token reply reached its first token in 77 ms, where without it a turn prefilled its reply again (2,300 tokens, 1.8 s).
- **Unsloth's GGUFs, all ten quants** from UD-IQ1_S to UD-Q8_K_XL, in Unsloth's own layout (the repository's root files, which `--model unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_M` selects) and in llama.cpp's converter's (the `.gguf_file`s of `Mainline_Support/`, from a local copy named `.gguf`). Each routed expert's `.scale` multiplies its products, whatever the experts' format, and UD-Q8_K_XL's BF16 experts of the last four layers load as MXFP8, lossless at half the bytes. AtomicChat's GGUFs, which keep the message gates quantized, load too.
- **Its release's weights**, `thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini`: NVFP4 experts with each expert's global scale in layers 0 to 19, MXFP8 in 20 to 23. Its BF16 attention projections and head load as Q8_0: on bf16 activations (`--activations bf16`), teacher-forced over 1,933 positions of five prompts, its logits are within a mean KL of 0.0024 nats of its BF16 projections' and pick the same top token at 98.7% of positions. On M5 and later Macs, by default, a prompt chunk of more than 64 rows runs those projections on int8 activations, which adds its own error (Int8-activation prefill in docs/internals.md).
- **Its MLX conversions**, in oMLX's, mlx-vlm's and mlx-lm's names, such as `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-4bit`, `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-oQ4e-mtp` and `nativ-community/Inkling-2-Mini-nvfp4`, the release's weights at their own precision in mlx-lm's names, which load into the release's images: a checkpoint and a GGUF that hold the same values load into the same image bytes.
- **The desktop app suggests it to Macs of 36 GB or more**, as `unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_M` (shell completion and the README list `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-4bit` too), each 18 GB with its draft. It serves text only.

## Decode

- **A layer's three mixer gates run as one call**: a message gate ran four dispatches, its rows' norm and concatenation, its down projection, its forget and gain projections and its gated row's norm. It now runs three fused kernels, which read the gates' weights as stored, F32 or BF16, and a gated application's q, k and v gates run in one call: a decode step's 96 gates run 144 dispatches, where they ran 384. Each value's arithmetic is unchanged, but the fused gates read their rows by quads, so a row's normalized input can differ from the four dispatches' by one bf16 step. On a 20-core M5 Pro, running the three as one call took a one-row cycle after a 2,048-token prompt from 29.15 to 28.85 ms and an eight-row one from 50.72 to 49.18, and reading by quads an eight-row cycle's gates from 4.1 to 2.9 ms in separate dispatches. The first kernel normalizes each row once for the down projection, whose 32 threadgroups a gate had each normalized their rows again, the same bits. On a 16-core M5 Pro, paired runs: an eight-row cycle takes 44.84 ms where it took 45.39 after a 47-token prompt (its gates 2.13 ms where they took 2.64, separate dispatches) and 48.31 where it took 48.61 after a 2,048-token one; a one-row cycle is level, 23.54 ms against 23.52 and 25.29 against 25.31; and the 47-token prompt's prefill takes 99.4 ms where it took 102.7.
- **A block residual mix runs a threadgroup a row and query** in a decode step, so a threadgroup waits on three reductions at most where one threadgroup a row took up to 25 sums in a row. Its sums add in another order: the teacher-forced target picks the oracle's greedy continuation of the five golden prompts at 99.0% of positions, against 98.9% before. On a 20-core M5 Pro a one-row cycle's mixes take 1.34 ms in separate dispatches where they took 3.10, an eight-row cycle's 3.72 where they took 4.80, and the one-row cycle 26.65 ms where it took 28.64.
- **A decode step's relative attention runs in one dispatch a layer**, where it ran a prompt chunk's three (prepare, split over each lane's history, reduce): after a 2,048-token prompt a one-row cycle ran 145 dispatches in them. On a 20-core M5 Pro that cycle's relative attention takes 2.31 ms over 49 dispatches where it took 2.93 over 145 (separate dispatches), and the cycle 27.76 ms where it took 28.96.
- **A decode lane's history splits fit the GPU's cores**: a lane takes a split per 80 / cores pages of history it reads (four on 20 cores), but one a page up to a verify block's most, and at most the edge measured on each GPU, 12 splits at the narrow cap and 7 at a verify block's on the 20-core M5 Pro, 16 and 10 on the 32-core M5 Max, and linearly in the cores between and beyond them. They had taken a split per four pages, at most 12 and 6, as the 20-core M5 Pro ran fastest. On that M5 Pro, one lane after a 2,048-token prompt, a cycle's relative attention takes 2.31 ms at one row where the earlier rule's took 2.46, and 3.46 at 16 rows where it took 3.76 (separate dispatches).
- **A fused projection's segments run as one dispatch on the column tile**, which a decode step of one or two live rows uses: Inkling-2-Mini's queries, gate and relative scores, and Qwen's attention q, k and v and GDN qkv, z and ab, each ran one dispatch a segment. Every value is the same bits. On a 16-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's one-row cycle after a 2,048-token prompt takes 26.52 ms where it took 26.64; the Qwen models' cycles are under Speed.
- **A decode step's FFN convolution runs in its MoE combine's dispatch**: Inkling-2-Mini's FFN output convolution, which ran as a dispatch of its own after the combine, now convolves each row along its path from the combined values in threadgroup memory. Every value is the same bits, Qwen's combine included. On a 20-core M5 Pro, paired runs after a 47-token prompt: a one-row cycle takes 0.07 ms less, an eight-row one 0.11 ms and a 16-row one 0.32 ms.
- **A decode step projects a row's keys and values as one, and convolves them in one dispatch**: where one row feeds both, as in 20 of Inkling-2-Mini's 40 sliding applications, its key and value projections run as one projection of their columns side by side, each tensor's columns at its own plan from one prepared input, and every application's key and value convolutions run in one dispatch, where each was a dispatch of its own. Every value is the same bits. On a 16-core M5 Pro, paired runs after a 47-token prompt: a one-row cycle takes 23.53 ms where it took 23.72, and an eight-row one 45.13 where it took 45.33, in 1,123 dispatches where it ran 1,211.
- **A 32-core M5 Max splits wide formats less**: on an Apple10 GPU of 32 cores or more, a projection of 6.25 bits a weight or more (Q6_K, Q8_0, MLX 6- and 8-bit, FP8) saturates a staged step from four threadgroups a core, where the wave law took six for every format and over-split these, among them Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's Q8_0 output projection and Inkling-2-Mini's fused Q8_0 attention input. Narrower formats keep six. These notes give no M5 Max numbers (Known issues).

## Prefill

- **A prompt chunk's block residual mixes run a threadgroup a row**: a call of more than 64 rows computes each row's state norms once for all its queries, where the decode change above made a 2,048-row chunk compute them once a query. A call of at most 64 rows, every one-lane decode step's among them, runs as in decode: the same kernel, grid and results. On a 20-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's prefill of 2,583 tokens takes 1,528 ms where it took 1,549 (-1.4%), and of 16K 10.01 s where it took 10.19 (-1.8%).
- **A prompt chunk's message gates run one tiled kernel**: a chunk of more rows than a decode step's ran each gate as four dispatches, each a pass over the chunk's rows (the rows' and messages' norms and concatenation, the down projection, the forget and gain projections and the gated rows' norm). A threadgroup now takes a gate's 32 rows from their rows to their output, the projections on simdgroup MMA, and a layer's q, k and v gates run in one call, as in decode. It rounds where the four dispatches did, but its sums add in another order, so a value can differ from theirs by one bf16 step. Every message gate now runs fused (a gate's four dispatches run only in the tests, as the reference the fused kernels are checked against), and a model whose gates the fused kernels cannot take, which no checkpoint known is, is refused at load. On a 16-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's prefill takes 2.4-3.4% less time from 200 to 16K tokens (its gates 102 ms of a 2,583-token prefill, where they took 141), and its logits, teacher-forced over 256 positions after a 4K and a 16K prefill, are within a mean KL of 1.2e-3 and 1.7e-3 of the build before's.
- **A prompt chunk's relative attention writes its rows in one pass where it reads its history in one split**: a chunk of more than 128 rows whose history fits one split runs 16-row tiles that write their gated rows themselves, where it ran 8-row tiles and a reduce dispatch after them. The rows' bits are the same. Chunks of more splits, and decode steps, run as in 1.7.1. On a 20-core M5 Pro a 2,583-token prefill's relative attention takes 213 ms where it took 240 (separate dispatches), and Inkling-2-Mini's prefill 1.8% less time at 2,583 tokens and 1.4-2.7% less at 16K.
- **An M5 Pro runs a prompt chunk's routed experts on 64-row tiles** where its routes average 64 or more an expert: an expert's weights stream once for up to 64 of its rows, where they streamed once for 32. A row's bits are the same on every tile. On a 20-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's chunks take them from 1,024 rows and the 35B's and the 122B's at 2,048; a 16-core M5 Pro runs them from 128 routes an expert, Inkling-2-Mini's 2,048-row chunks, as there the 35B's 2,048-row chunks ran 2.2-2.5% slower on them. Flint's and Flash-Next's never do. Other GPUs keep 32-row tiles: on an M6 and a 10-core M4 the 64-row tiles were no faster, and the M5 Max is unmeasured. On a 20-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's prefill takes 3.1% less time at 4K and at 32K tokens.
- **A GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations** on M5 and later Macs, in prompt chunks of more than 64 rows, as MLX's 4- and 5-bit ones already did: Q8_0's int8 weights meet the activations' int8 codes on the GPU's int8 path, each 32-weight block folding into fp32 with its own scale. Within its tests the tile's error is 1.009 to 1.024 times the bf16 tile's (bound 1.25). Inkling-2-Mini's attention projections and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's GDN and attention projections take it; Qwen3.8-27B's UD-Q4_K_M keeps the bf16 tile, as its Q8_0 k and v share a projection with a q of another format. On a 20-core M5 Pro, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's prefill takes 3.8% less time at 512 tokens and 4.1% at 4K, and Inkling-2-Mini's 1.3% at 512 and 1.5% at 32K.

## Models and formats

- **MXFP8** is a format of the format list, lossless at 8.25 bits a weight: MLX's mode `mxfp8`, a release's MXFP8 experts, and a GGUF's BF16 or F16 routed experts whose values are exactly MXFP8's. Every start reads those experts' values to check them: on a 48 GB M5 Pro, the 8.15 GB of such experts in Inkling-2-Mini's UD-Q8_K_XL take 0.43 s with the file in the page cache and 1.05 s from a fresh copy. A Mac that streams such GGUF experts reads their 16-bit values at every load, twice MXFP8's SSD bytes.
- **Each routed expert's `.scale`** multiplies its gate, up and down products in every expert format, as llama.cpp does, in every MoE model's image: a GGUF's `.scale`, an MLX experts module's global scales and Inkling-2-Mini's release's per-expert scales all go there. A checkpoint of one module an expert (Model Optimizer's and llm-compressor's NVFP4, such as `nvidia/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4`) keeps each expert's tensor scale in its NVFP4 rows, as before.
- **NVFP4 rows of a multiple of 64 inputs but not of 256** load in NVFP4's 64-element blocks, from a GGUF or a checkpoint.
- **A checkpoint's BF16 or F16 projections, experts and head load as Q8_0**, by the reference Q8_0 quantization, where they were refused: releases that quantize their experts alone, and RedHatAI's, whose head is BF16. Experts whose values are exactly MXFP8's load as MXFP8.
- **An MLX nvfp4 module's global scale** (`global_scale` or `weight_scale_2`) scales its rows, and an experts module's per-expert ones its experts, in every family's checkpoints. They were dropped before (Upgrade).
- **A family's float-projection tensors quantized in a GGUF** (Inkling-2-Mini's message gates, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's indexer projections) load as their F32 values, where they were refused.

## Server and API

- **Whether a request's effort reaches the model and whether it turns the reasoning on and off are two facts of a chat format now.** Inkling 2's template tells the model the effort, but the model opens its reasoning itself and reasons at `none` too; `/status` reports `reasoning_switch` false for it, and the thinking budget's options act on no Inkling 2 model, as on no Flint model. Nothing changes for the Qwen families or Flint.

## Fixes

- **A new conversation whose head ends differently resumes at the last prefill checkpoint below its head**, where it prefilled its whole prompt. An agent's head ends in text of its own, its system prompt's environment block (the working directory, the git status) after the tools, so a new session in another directory, or in the same one once its git status has changed, first differs inside the head's last page, past the state kept there, and publishing that state retired the lane's last checkpoint below it. The head's state now keeps that checkpoint cached, as the replay point keeps its own, so such a conversation prefills again at most 4,096 tokens of its head, the checkpoints' spacing, or less than 6,144 where the first conversation's prompt ends within 2,048 tokens past the multiple of 4,096 below its head, which then holds no checkpoint. With Claude Code on Inkling-2-Mini, whose head is 27.5K tokens, that is about 3K of them. On the 16-core M5 Pro, at that run's prefill rate past 24K tokens, a new session in another directory prefills about 5.2K of its 29.8K tokens, about 4.6 s, where it took 27 to 33 s to its first token, and a new conversation in the same directory, whose 32,051 tokens took 25.7 s, prefills 7,475, about 6.6 s. Each head keeps one more cached state, a checkpoint, which reclaim takes first: 4.4 MiB for Inkling-2-Mini, 19.6 MiB for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 46.9 MiB for Qwen3.8-27B.
- **The app's Text only reaches its installer** (#796): a GGUF repository without a vision projector, of a family that serves images, installs from the app with Text only on, where the download was refused for want of the projector, and the app lists a model installed with Text only alone and deletes every installation of a model, its Text only one included.
- **A `background` request runs as `normal` once it has been in the engine for 10 s**, where strict priority kept it waiting while attended work ran: a run a user asked for at background priority waited 14.5 minutes for 9 tool calls on a busy 64 GB Mac, its start refused while normal lanes ran and its decode never picked beside theirs. A title or a summary ends well inside 10 s and still yields. It still yields memory and its lane as `background`: a `normal` start that the lanes or memory cannot hold beside it suspends it, and suspended it goes back to `background` and waits another 10 s, so it does not decode beside the start it gave way to.
- **A lane suspended in a long reply resumes at its reply's newest state**, where it replayed the whole reply. A lane that a higher priority's start or the memory wall suspends kept its reply's states opportunistic, so the first reclaim dropped the newest, and the resume replayed the reply from its prompt's replay point, every token of a long reasoning reply. A suspended lane whose reply holds 2,048 tokens or more past that point now keeps its newest state, as an ended reply that its next turn renders back keeps its last, in every family that captures reply states in decode and whatever the client: a reclaim meanwhile writes it to the SSD cache once, with the pages of its chain that have no copy there, and the lane's next capture after its resume retires it. A shorter reply recomputes faster than its state writes, and its states are dropped as before. It costs at most one state's write a suspension: 4.4 MiB for Inkling-2-Mini, 19.6 MiB for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 46.9 MiB for Qwen3.8-27B.
- **The persistent SSD cache keeps a head's state across a restart**: a prompt's head, its system prompt and tools, keeps a state at its end where it lies 2,048 tokens or more past where the request resumes, which write-behind passed over, as it copies only a conversation's newest point and every conversation goes on from its head. Only a reclaim wrote it to the SSD, so after a restart or a crash the next conversation prefilled the head again: 17.3 s for a 9.7K-token agent head on Qwen3.8-27B. With `--persistent-cache`, write-behind now copies the head's state 10 s after its publication, as it copies a replay point, while it stays an ordinary state; the checkpoint the head keeps below it is not copied. It costs one state's write for each distinct head: 4.4 MiB for Inkling-2-Mini, 19.6 MiB for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and 46.9 MiB for Qwen3.8-27B, its KV being the replay point's, which is copied anyway. A client whose head differs in every conversation pays it for each.
- **A start whose KV extents are shorter than its runway no longer stops at the Neural Engine split's set-up** (#806): since 1.7.1 the split's warmup held the whole startup runway again after the first memory audit had given an empty extent back, and the start stopped with "cannot retain a KV page of an unallocated extent", as Qwen3.8-27B GGUF does with `--kv-format bf16` (59-page extents) and a plan of 64-page extents can with five to eight lanes. The late warmup now holds only the pages its rows use and allocates a released extent among them again; on a 48 GB M5 Pro that 27B start reaches Ready where it stopped.

## Upgrade

```bash
brew upgrade splash
```

Keep the CLI and desktop app on the same release.

- **A MoE model's persistent SSD cache starts cold once**: each MoE layer's image gains a section, its experts' scales, so the images' sizes change and with them the namespace of the cache `--persistent-cache` keeps (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B and Ornith-1.5-122B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Flint). The section's multiply costs no measurable time.
- **Each model's first start measures its decode step costs again**, and calibrates its Neural Engine split again where it has one: a start keeps both for its engine's build, as after every release.
- **An MLX nvfp4 checkpoint that holds global scales** now applies them, so its outputs change: 1.7.1 dropped them.
- **On an M5 Max** (an Apple10 GPU of 32 cores or more), a decode step splits Q6_K, Q8_0, MLX 6- and 8-bit and FP8 projections less (Decode), so their partitions sum in another order: outputs there can differ from 1.7.1's at near ties.
- **On M5 and later Macs, a GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations** in prompt chunks of more than 64 rows (Prefill), so a long prompt's outputs can differ from 1.7.1's where a model has such projections; `--activations bf16` keeps them on bf16.
- **The engine protocol stays at version 15** and its status schema at 8.

## Known issues

- **The round of Metal test suites on a 32-core M5 Max did not finish** for this release: the Mac went offline 14 suites into its 54, on a candidate before this release's last changes, so none of them ran on the released code there. Its kernel choices for that GPU (the staged splits of wide formats, the decode history splits) are not validated on it by the full round, and these notes give no M5 Max numbers.
- **Inkling-2-Mini serves text only**: its vision tower is not served yet, and its MTP head is not loaded (its DFlash2 draft drafts).
- **Inkling-2-Mini reasons at `reasoning_effort` `none`** too, 50 to 140 tokens on a one-word question, and `--thinking-token-budget`, `--thinking-end-text` and a request's `thinking_token_budget` do not act on it. A client must leave room for the reasoning in a request's output limit, which counts its tokens: a short limit can end a response before its answer starts.
- **`gguf-projection` can fail at random under Metal shader validation on an M5 Pro** (#807), as since 1.7.1: the validator reads some of the staged tile's in-bounds weight prefetches, loaded behind a condition, as zero. Outputs without validation are right. Loading them on every step passes there but fails the suite under validation on an M4, so 1.7.2 keeps 1.7.1's loads.
- **Inkling-2-Mini on 24 GB Macs** is not measured; the desktop app suggests it from 36 GB.
