# Splash 1.7.2

Splash 1.7.2 serves Inkling-2-Mini with its DFlash2 draft, `incoai/Inkling-2-Mini-DFlash2`, from every one of Unsloth's GGUF quants, from its release's weights and from their MLX conversions; and decodes it faster: a layer's mixer gates run as one call, a block residual mix runs a threadgroup a query, relative attention runs in one pass a layer with history splits fitted to each GPU's cores, a fused projection runs as one dispatch, and a 32-core M5 Max splits wide formats less. It also prefills faster: a prompt chunk's block residual mixes run a threadgroup a row, a one-split chunk's relative attention writes its rows in one pass, an M5 Pro runs a chunk's routed experts on 64-row tiles, and on M5 and later a GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations.

## Inkling-2-Mini

- **A new family**: Inkling-2-Mini (`thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini`) is a decoder of 24 weight layers over a MoE of 128 experts, eight a token, whose layers 6 to 17 run three times a token, 48 layer applications, each with its own KV and convolution state. Its attention has no RoPE: a relative bias, a sink, an output gate and a window of 1,024 keys on 20 of its 24 layers. Short convolutions follow its keys, its values and every mixer's and FFN's output, a block residual mixes the inputs of layers 0, 6, 12 and 18 into every mixer, FFN and the head, and its logits are softcapped. It is served up to 1,010,000 tokens, below its own window of 1,048,576, in its own chat format, its reasoning and tool calls included, with the sampling its release states: temperature 1.0 over every token.
- **Its DFlash2 draft**, `incoai/Inkling-2-Mini-DFlash2`, installs with it, as every family's draft does. It reads the rows applications 5, 23 and 41 read and the final norm's, and finishes its candidates' logits as its target's head does. [TODO: the draft's revision on the Hub.]
- **Unsloth's GGUFs, all ten quants** from UD-IQ1_S to UD-Q8_K_XL, in Unsloth's own layout (the repository's root files, which `--model unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_M` selects) and in llama.cpp's converter's (the `.gguf_file`s of `Mainline_Support/`, from a local copy named `.gguf`). Each routed expert's `.scale` multiplies its products, whatever the experts' format, and UD-Q8_K_XL's BF16 experts of the last four layers load as MXFP8, lossless at half the bytes. AtomicChat's GGUFs, which keep the message gates quantized, load too.
- **Its release's weights**, `thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini`: NVFP4 experts with each expert's global scale in layers 0 to 19, MXFP8 in 20 to 23. Its BF16 attention projections and head load as Q8_0: on bf16 activations (`--activations bf16`), teacher-forced over 1,933 positions of five prompts, its logits are within a mean KL of 0.0024 nats of its BF16 projections' and pick the same top token at 98.7% of positions. On M5 and later Macs, by default, a prompt chunk of more than 64 rows runs those projections on int8 activations, which adds its own error (Int8-activation prefill in docs/internals.md).
- **Its MLX conversions**, in oMLX's, mlx-vlm's and mlx-lm's names, such as `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-4bit`, `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-oQ4e-mtp` and `nativ-community/Inkling-2-Mini-nvfp4`, the release's weights at their own precision in mlx-lm's names, which load into the release's images: a checkpoint and a GGUF that hold the same values load into the same image bytes.
- **The desktop app suggests it to Macs of 36 GB or more**, as `unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF:UD-Q4_K_M` (shell completion and the README list `mlx-community/Inkling-2-Mini-4bit` too), each 18 GB with its draft. It serves text only.

## Decode

- **A layer's three mixer gates run as one call**: a message gate ran four dispatches, its rows' norm and concatenation, its down projection, its forget and gain projections and its gated row's norm. It now runs two fused kernels, which read the gates' weights as stored, F32 or BF16, and a gated application's q, k and v gates run in one call: a decode step's 96 gates run 96 dispatches, where they ran 384. Each value's arithmetic is unchanged, but the fused gates read their rows by quads, so a row's normalized input can differ from the float projections' path by one bf16 step. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A block residual mix runs a threadgroup a row and query** in a decode step, so a threadgroup waits on three reductions at most where one threadgroup a row took up to 25 sums in a row. Its sums add in another order: the teacher-forced target picks the oracle's greedy continuation of the five golden prompts at 99.0% of positions, against 98.9% before. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A decode step's relative attention runs in one dispatch a layer**, where it ran a prompt chunk's three (prepare, split over each lane's history, reduce): after a 2,048-token prompt a one-row cycle ran 145 dispatches in them. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A decode lane's history splits fit the GPU's cores**: a lane takes a split per 80 / cores pages of history it reads (four on 20 cores), but one a page up to a verify block's most, and at most the edge measured on each GPU, 12 splits at the narrow cap and 7 at a verify block's on the 20-core M5 Pro, 16 and 10 on the 32-core M5 Max, and linearly in the cores between and beyond them. They had taken a split per four pages, at most 12 and 6, as the 20-core M5 Pro ran fastest. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A fused projection's segments run as one dispatch on the column tile**, which a decode step of one or two live rows uses: Inkling-2-Mini's queries, gate and relative scores, and Qwen's attention q, k and v and GDN qkv, z and ab, each ran one dispatch a segment. Every value is the same bits. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's and Qwen's.]
- **A decode step's FFN convolution runs in its MoE combine's dispatch**: Inkling-2-Mini's FFN output convolution, which ran as a dispatch of its own after the combine, now convolves each row along its path from the combined values in threadgroup memory. Every value is the same bits, Qwen's combine included. [TODO: cycle times on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A 32-core M5 Max splits wide formats less**: on an Apple10 GPU of 32 cores or more, a projection of 6.25 bits a weight or more (Q6_K, Q8_0, MLX 6- and 8-bit, FP8) saturates a staged step from four threadgroups a core, where the wave law took six for every format and over-split these, among them Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's Q8_0 output projection and Inkling-2-Mini's fused Q8_0 attention input. Narrower formats keep six. These notes give no M5 Max numbers (Known issues).

## Prefill

- **A prompt chunk's block residual mixes run a threadgroup a row**: a call of more than 64 rows computes each row's state norms once for all its queries, where the decode change above made a 2,048-row chunk compute them once a query. A call of at most 64 rows, every one-lane decode step's among them, runs as in decode: the same kernel, grid and results. [TODO: the 2,048-row chunk's mixes and the 16K prefill against the build without it, on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A prompt chunk's relative attention writes its rows in one pass where it reads its history in one split**: a chunk of more than 128 rows whose history fits one split runs 16-row tiles that write their gated rows themselves, where it ran 8-row tiles and a reduce dispatch after them. The rows' bits are the same. Chunks of more splits, and decode steps, run as in 1.7.1. [TODO: the relative attention's and the prefill's time at 2,048 tokens and 16K on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **An M5 Pro runs a prompt chunk's routed experts on 64-row tiles** where its routes average 64 or more an expert: an expert's weights stream once for up to 64 of its rows, where they streamed once for 32. A row's bits are the same on every tile. On a 20-core M5 Pro, Inkling-2-Mini's chunks take them from 1,024 rows and the 35B's and the 122B's at 2,048; a 16-core M5 Pro runs them from 128 routes an expert, Inkling-2-Mini's 2,048-row chunks, as there the 35B's 2,048-row chunks ran 2.2-2.5% slower on them. Flint's and Flash-Next's never do. Other GPUs keep 32-row tiles: on an M6 and a 10-core M4 the 64-row tiles were no faster, and the M5 Max is unmeasured. [TODO: Inkling-2-Mini's prefill at 4K and 32K on the 16-core M5 Pro.]
- **A GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations** on M5 and later Macs, in prompt chunks of more than 64 rows, as MLX's 4- and 5-bit ones already did: Q8_0's int8 weights meet the activations' int8 codes on the GPU's int8 path, each 32-weight block folding into fp32 with its own scale. Within its tests the tile's error is 1.009 to 1.024 times the bf16 tile's (bound 1.25). Inkling-2-Mini's attention projections and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's GDN and attention projections take it; Qwen3.8-27B's UD-Q4_K_M keeps the bf16 tile, as its Q8_0 k and v share a projection with a q of another format. ## Models and formats

- **MXFP8** is a format of the format list, lossless at 8.25 bits a weight: MLX's mode `mxfp8`, a release's MXFP8 experts, and a GGUF's BF16 or F16 routed experts whose values are exactly MXFP8's. Every start reads those experts' values to check them: on a 48 GB M5 Pro, the 8.15 GB of such experts in Inkling-2-Mini's UD-Q8_K_XL take 0.43 s with the file in the page cache and 1.05 s from a fresh copy. A Mac that streams such GGUF experts reads their 16-bit values at every load, twice MXFP8's SSD bytes.
- **Each routed expert's `.scale`** multiplies its gate, up and down products in every expert format, as llama.cpp does, in every MoE model's image; NVFP4's tensor scale of its experts is no longer a case of its own.
- **NVFP4 rows of a multiple of 64 inputs but not of 256** load in NVFP4's 64-element blocks, from a GGUF or a checkpoint.
- **A checkpoint's BF16 or F16 projections, experts and head load as Q8_0**, by the reference Q8_0 quantization, where they were refused: releases that quantize their experts alone, and RedHatAI's, whose head is BF16. Experts whose values are exactly MXFP8's load as MXFP8.
- **An MLX nvfp4 module's global scale** (`global_scale` or `weight_scale_2`) scales its rows, and an experts module's per-expert ones its experts, in every family's checkpoints. They were dropped before (Upgrade).
- **A family's float-projection tensors quantized in a GGUF** (Inkling-2-Mini's message gates, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's indexer projections) load as their F32 values, where they were refused.

## Server and API

- **Whether a request's effort reaches the model and whether it turns the reasoning on and off are two facts of a chat format now.** Inkling 2's template tells the model the effort, but the model opens its reasoning itself and reasons at `none` too; `/status` reports `reasoning_switch` false for it, and the thinking budget's options act on no Inkling 2 model, as on no Flint model. Nothing changes for the Qwen families or Flint.

## Upgrade

```bash
brew upgrade splash
```

Keep the CLI and desktop app on the same release.

- **A MoE model's persistent SSD cache starts cold once**: each MoE layer's image gains a section, its experts' scales, so the images' sizes change and with them the namespace of the cache `--persistent-cache` keeps (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B and Ornith-1.5-122B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Flint). The section's multiply costs no measurable time. [TODO: whatever else a start keeps for a model and measures again once, if anything.]
- **An MLX nvfp4 checkpoint that holds global scales** now applies them, so its outputs change: 1.7.1 dropped them.
- **On an M5 Max** (an Apple10 GPU of 32 cores or more), a decode step splits Q6_K, Q8_0, MLX 6- and 8-bit and FP8 projections less (Decode), so their partitions sum in another order: outputs there can differ from 1.7.1's at near ties.
- **On M5 and later Macs, a GGUF's Q8_0 projections prefill on int8 activations** in prompt chunks of more than 64 rows (Prefill), so a long prompt's outputs can differ from 1.7.1's where a model has such projections; `--activations bf16` keeps them on bf16.
- **The engine protocol stays at version 15** and its status schema at 8.

## Known issues

- **The round of Metal test suites on a 32-core M5 Max did not finish** for this release. A client must leave room for the reasoning in a request's output limit, which counts its tokens: a short limit can end a response before its answer starts.
