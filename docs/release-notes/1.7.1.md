# Splash 1.7.1

Splash 1.7.1 runs a large prompt's FFN on the Neural Engine while the GPU runs the other half's attention or GDN layers; takes a concurrency limit of one to eight, four by default; resumes a new conversation at the end of the system prompt and tools it shares with an earlier one; loads weights and starts sooner; runs a resident MLX MoE model's prefill experts on int8 activations; and loads Flint and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next from MLX checkpoints.

## Speed

Against 1.7.0, runs of each build in turn.

- **Neural Engine prefill**, Qwen3.8-27B, each build at its own calibration: on a 24 GB M6 (`UD-IQ3_XXS`) prompts of 1,024 to 2,016 tokens reach their first token 14.1% to 23.1% sooner than with 1.7.0's split, and cold 2K and 8K prompts 23.7% and 22.8% sooner; from 1,024 tokens, 6.4% to 12.6% sooner on a 16-core M5 Pro (`UD-Q4_K_M`), 1.1% to 5.1% on a 20-core M5 Pro and 0.8% to 2.6% on an M5 Max (MLX 4-bit), whose faster GPUs leave the Neural Engine less to take. Prompts of 512 to 1,023 tokens are 1% to 5% sooner on the M6 and level on the others.
- **Decode is level with 1.7.0**: a step's GPU time at one to four lanes moves between 0.2% less and 0.3% more (Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit and `UD-Q4_K_M`, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q4_K_M`), and a served request that decodes the same answer takes between 0.2% less and 0.1% more time a token at one lane (Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit, 2K and 31K prompts, 64 GB M5 Pro).
- **Eight lanes against four**, eight clients at once on a 64 GB M5 Pro: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q4_K_M` serves 1.13× the tokens a second in sum on a 2K document (176 to 180 against 154 to 161) and every first token comes within 6.8 s where four lanes took up to 9.8; Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit 1.02× to 1.03×; both level at 31K.
- **A second conversation with an earlier one's system prompt and tools** reaches its first token 2.9× sooner on Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit (a 2,880-token head and a 1,500-token message of its own on a 48 GB M5 Pro: 1.87 s where it took 5.52), 2.9× on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q4_K_M` and 2.5× on Flint (64 GB M5 Pro); with a head of about 9K tokens, of which 1.7.0 restored 8,192, 1.6× to 2.2×.
- **Weight loads**, files in the file cache, on a 32-core M5 Max: Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit 1.29×, Qwen3.8-27B `UD-Q4_K_M` 1.54×, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q4_K_M` 1.34×. Flint's weights load from its `UD-Q4_K_XL` GGUF in 3.3 s, where reading a shared expert's window a row at a time took 11.1 s (64 GB M5 Pro).
- **Starts**: from spawn to ready 1.16× to 1.62× (8.3 to 8.4 s for those three models), and the first request after an idle release reaches its first token 1.38× to 1.53× sooner; both measured before the Neural Engine change, whose effect on starts is under Upgrade.
- **MoE prefill from MLX**: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MLX 4-bit prefills a cold prompt 1.04× faster at 480 tokens to 1.11× at 2,016, and 14,096 tokens 1.10× (M5 Max).
- **Experts streamed from the SSD**: a streamed expert's repack runs a 64-row tile's span of groups a threadgroup, so a Qwen3.8-Flash-Next prefill with streamed experts is 1.05× faster at 2K tokens and 1.07× at 8K on a 24 GB M6, about 1.03× on a 48 GB M5 Pro.
- **Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B** takes 158 ms for a four-lane decode step of 16 rows a lane where it took 212 on a 24 GB M6, 1.34×; a one-lane step keeps 1.7.0's time (20-core M5 Pro).
- **Slower than 1.7.0**: a default four-lane decode cycle takes 0.10% to 1.01% longer on identical work (Qwen3.8-27B MLX 4-bit and `UD-Q4_K_M`, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MLX 4-bit; 20-core and 16-core M5 Pro): the eight-lane kernels' lane selects and wider bindings. A later start takes 0.6 to 2.0 s longer (Upgrade). A cold prompt tokenizes 0.4% to 3.4% slower with the reuse of tokenized messages (#738).

## Neural Engine

- **Commands of 1,024 rows or more run in two pipelined steps**: the Neural Engine runs one half's FFN while the GPU runs the other half's attention or GDN layers, at a larger share of its own. Commands of 512 to 1,023 rows run one step as in 1.7.0, and shorter ones the GPU alone. A dense model's prefill command is two chunks of 1,024 rows.
- **One calibration, as the warmup's last step**: two steps' share comes from the same fit as one step's, one more timing and the GPU time of the engine's own first warmup prefill; the split is set up after the warmup has given its memory back.
- **A calibration taken on a busier Mac is taken again**: a start that recalls one compares its own warmup's time with the record's and calibrates again where the record's is more than a tenth longer.
- **`--recalibrate-ane` calibrates again on request**: a start with it takes no kept calibration and keeps what it finds. It is for a Mac whose calibration was taken while another app used the Neural Engine, which the busy check does not see. The app has it on the Server page as Recalibrate Neural Engine.
- **A calibration can no longer keep a false "no gain"** (#792): each share is timed right after its own reading of the GPU alone, where one reading taken before the Neural Engine's programs compiled was used for all (an M4 MacBook Air's GPU read 352.9 ms a layer first and 470 once settled, and its fit ended 1.2% short of the gain it needs); and a calibration that finds no gain is not remembered, so the next start measures again (an M3 Ultra that calibrated beside two serving engines had kept "no split" for every later start).
- **Gate and up run in blocks of 1,024 channels on the M6 and later**, where the Neural Engine runs them faster; other Macs run them whole, as before.
- **The split's requests run at user-initiated QoS**, so another client's user-initiated work no longer queues ahead of them: beside such a client each evaluation reported 234 ms after the GPU asked instead of 12.4.
- **A split that loses pauses after one command** for five minutes instead of losing over windows of eight commands, then probes; a probe that loses stops it for the session, and its calibration stays. Beside a second client on a 20-core M5 Pro, 40 cold 8K prompts took 10.2 to 10.8 s each, where 1.7.0 left the first at 147 s.
- **A stalled command logs one warning** naming the Neural Engine or the GPU, a split command slowed by another process's work on the GPU says so, and a command is submitted before its evaluations are queued, so the GPU works while the Neural Engine wakes: first tokens after an idle come 26 to 94 ms sooner.

## Concurrency

- **A concurrency limit of one to eight**: `--max-concurrent-requests N`, four by default, and "Concurrency limit" on the app's Server page. Memory is planned for the lanes asked for; a default start is 1.7.0's in memory, context and outputs. Where memory bounds the context, a start of more than four lanes says what they cost in its log, in `/status` (`memory_plan.budget.default_lanes_context_tokens`) and in the app: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B `UD-Q2_K_XL` on a 24 GB M6 with `--max-memory 13G --expert-memory 7G` serves 155,633 tokens at eight lanes, where four leave 188,401.
- **A decode step holds a verify block of 16 rows a lane**, 128 rows at eight lanes. Past 64 rows its projections run two equal row groups of the decode tile in one dispatch, and their precision does not change with the step's rows: bf16 activations on MLX and GGUF weights, int8 on Ternary-Bonsai's from 16 rows.
- **A target whose experts stream** keeps each step `--verify-rows auto` chooses, and each warm-up, within the expert store's 32-row round: eight lanes on a 16 GB M4 decode at 8.15 ms a token where steps past the round took 9.06.

## Prefill and caches

- **A state at a shared head** (#361, #706): a new conversation that opens with a system or developer message or tools gets a cached state at the end of that head on its first prefill, where the head holds no image. The next conversation with the same head resumes there, from memory, or from the SSD once a reclaim has written it. It is an ordinary cached state, kept and evicted as any other.
- **Int8 MoE prefill**: a resident MLX 4-bit or 5-bit MoE target runs its routed and shared experts on int8 activations in the prefill chunks whose routes average six or more an expert (from 192 rows on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), by the rule its dense projections already use. Decode, smaller chunks, streamed experts, GGUF experts and Apple9 GPUs keep bf16, and `--activations bf16` keeps every plan exact.
- **A streamed expert's repack** runs a 64-row tile's span of groups a threadgroup: a prefill command's experts cost 1.1 to 1.35 times a copy of their bytes, where they cost 4 times. An MLX checkpoint's streamed expert is read as the planes it stores and repacked on the GPU, where its rows were built on the CPU.
- **A start checks the step costs it recalls against the Mac's level**: it replays its largest step and measures every table again where a kept one is more than a fifth slower, so a table measured beside another GPU user no longer steers later starts.

## Decode

- **Ternary-Bonsai decodes in int8 at every step** of 16 rows or more on M5 and later, its vocabulary heads included, where steps of three and four lanes ran bf16; against those, next-token top-1 agreement 0.990 and 1.000 at 48 and 64 rows, mean KL 2.4e-4 and 9.3e-5.
- **The paged attention kernel** tests a page against each row's window, causal end and tree path only where some row of the tile does not see it whole. Output bits are unchanged; in one layer at 32K and 98K histories, prefill and verify are 1.4% to 3.9% faster on a 16-core M5 Pro.

## Models

- **Flint from MLX**: `mlx-community/flint-mixed-3-4bit`, 18 GB with its draft against 26 GB for the `UD-Q4_K_XL` GGUF, is offered to 36 GB Macs. Its experts are 3-bit: top-1 agreement with the GGUF 0.85. On a 64 GB M5 Pro it decodes at 17.8 ms a cycle where the GGUF takes 23.5, and prefills 2,048 tokens in 419 ms against 490.
- **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next from MLX**: `mlx-community/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-4bit`, with its DFlash2 draft and vision; its PLE table streams from the checkpoint. Its experts are 2.2 times the Q2_0 GGUF's bytes, so with experts streamed it decodes 1.7 to 3 times slower than the GGUF (48 GB M5 Pro).
- **An MLX model without a vision tower** installs without `--language-only`.
- **The vision tower** runs on the shared dense operators: an image of 64x64 patches encodes 1.13× faster on an M6, and no measured size is slower.

## Server and API

- **`--sampling-default NAME=VALUE`**, repeatable, sets a sampling field a request leaves out: temperature, top_p, top_k, min_p and the three penalties (#614, Peter van Velzen).
- **A branch, a regenerated answer or a new conversation with a cached one's system prompt** reuses the tokens of the whole messages it shares: a 13.8K-token branch tokenizes in 0.30 ms where it took 9.9 (#738, David Kornahrens).
- **Usage** gains `prefix_tokens`, `state_source` (`"memory"`, `"disk"` or null) and `lost_state`, so a client can tell why a turn was slow.
- **`/status`** gains `disk.off_reason` and the SSD reads of spilled K/V and of streamed weights, which `/metrics` exports too; `/v1/models` gains `limits.image_aspect`.
- **The server spawns the engine** as soon as the model's family and options are checked, and builds the grammars' table while the engine loads.

## The Mac app

- **Recalibrate Neural Engine** on the Server page, below Neural Engine for long prompts: while it is on, each start calibrates the Neural Engine's share again.
- **Unsent drafts**, text and attachments, come back as the app opens again.
- **Downloads** start their progress at the bytes the Hub cache holds, refuse a disk without room for the model and its draft before the first byte, and say a full disk or a dropped connection in the app's words.
- **The chat page** aligns table columns, shows bold beside Chinese, Japanese and Korean text, shows a streaming table as a table from its first frame, shows formulas as written and makes a bare `https` or `mailto` address a link.
- **The SSD Cache options** say why the engine turned off a cache they asked for; **a refused launch option** is named as the Server page names it; **a long model name** keeps its own name and loses its end.
- **The packaged runtime ships its Python bytecode**: the server's imports take 112 ms where they took 1,121 (M5 Max).

## Fixes

- **Streamed `json_schema` requests no longer fail mid-stream with "verify mask is not synchronized to the pending anchor"** (#790; about 1% of such requests with reasoning off since 1.7.0, 5% of long nested answers). The grammar library keeps the last mask it computed under a key that another branch of a draft tree can reach again after tokens are taken back; the server now walks each branch on a copy of its parent's matcher and takes no token back. With Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's tokenizer and a nested-array schema, 40,000 simulated cycles showed 4 such failures before and none after.
- **A Neural Engine calibration that found no gain once is no longer kept for good** (#792; above).
- **The automatic Metal budget leaves a tenth of the Mac's memory unwired** (#735): on a 128 GB M5 Max whose GPU wired limit had been raised, the engine and two other GPU processes wired 96% of the memory and the Mac panicked. Macs of 16 to 128 GB at their default wired limit are unaffected; `--max-memory` can still go past it, up to the GPU's recommendation.
- **A start of a model from the app** runs the runtime's commands with the app's own home, so a model started from an app opened with another `HOME` shows ready.

## Upgrade

```bash
brew upgrade splash
```

Keep the CLI and desktop app on the same release.

- **A resident MLX MoE model's prefill** rounds its experts' activations to int8 in chunks of 192 rows or more, as its dense projections already did: against 1.7.0 on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, prefixes shorter than 192 tokens give identical logits and longer ones keep their most likely next token, mean KL 1.4e-3. The int8 passes take 45 MiB of scratch at a 2,048-row chunk. `--activations bf16` keeps the exact path.
- **Ternary-Bonsai at three and four lanes** decodes in int8 on M5 and later: outputs can differ at near ties.
- **A new conversation with a system prompt or tools caches one more state**, at its head's end: 46.9 MiB on Qwen3.8-27B, 19.6 MiB on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.
- **A start whose recalled step costs are more than a fifth above the Mac's level measures them again**, once. More than four lanes warm four more step widths: about 14 s more at a start on a 24 GB M6.
- **The packaged runtime's archive** grows from 80.4 MB to 102.9 MB with its bytecode.
- **The first start after the upgrade calibrates the Neural Engine split once more** (its record's form changed). With the programs compiled before, it reaches ready in 14.1 s on a 20-core M5 Pro, 21.5 s on a 16-core and 14.3 s on an M5 Max, where 1.7.0's took 15.0, 22.4 and 30.5. A start that calibrates reads the GPU alone twice more: about 0.4 s on an M5 Max, 0.6 to 1.1 s on an M5 Pro, 1.5 s on an M6, 7 to 11 s on an M4. A first start that compiles its programs took 30.4 s on the 20-core M5 Pro, 32.3 on the M5 Max and 79.8 on a 24 GB M6.
- **A later start takes 0.6 to 2.0 s longer than 1.7.0's**: 7.6 against 7.0 s on an M5 Max, 8.1 against 7.3 on a 20-core M5 Pro, 11.5 against 9.5 on a 16-core. Its first warmup prefill runs on the GPU alone, for the time the busy check reads, and a prefill of the split's least rows creates its kernels.
- **The memory plan holds the largest Neural Engine split a model could take**, so that every start of a model on a Mac serves the same context. On a Mac whose memory bounds the context this takes context: on a 24 GB M6 with Qwen3.8-27B `UD-IQ3_XXS` the automatic context is 81,905 tokens with 1.7.1, 86,001 with 1.7.0 and 98,289 with `--disable-ane`. Macs whose context the model's window bounds keep it.
- **More of each FFN runs in the Neural Engine's int8** where two steps take a larger share: against the GPU alone in bf16 activations on Qwen3.8-27B, next-token KL is about 9e-4 on an M5 Max and on a 16-core M5 Pro (1.7.0's split: 7e-4 and 4e-4) and 4e-3 on an M6 (1.7.0's: 3e-3); the top token is unchanged at every decided prefix. `--ane-ffn-share 0` keeps the GPU alone.
- **A Neural Engine split that loses pauses for five minutes** instead of stopping until the engine restarts, and a stop no longer makes the next start calibrate again.

## Known issues

- **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with a starved expert store** can verify whole blocks after a cold cycle, as in 1.6.2 and 1.7.0, and takes 20% to 30% more time a token meanwhile: in forced runs it stayed so for about 400 tokens on both M5 Pros and did not leave within 150 cycles on a 24 GB M6. It was seen in 1 of 26 starved one-lane sessions measured.
- **A prompt whose uncached part is 65 to 127 tokens on GGUF weights** runs the prefill tile, as in 1.7.0: on a 24 GB M6 with Qwen3.8-27B `UD-IQ3_XXS` a 72-token follow-up takes 381 ms of GPU where a 63-token one takes 211.
- **On a 24 GB M6 with Qwen3.8-27B `UD-IQ3_XXS`**, a request whose uncached part is 64 to 127 tokens can reach its first token 1 to 2 ms (about 0.4%) later with the Neural Engine split: the engine's start places one of its buffers differently, not added work.
- **On a Mac at its memory limit the Neural Engine split may not engage.** On a 16 GB M4 with Qwen3.8-27B and its draft, the last allocation of a start is refused when macOS can reclaim no more than the engine protects: in 1.7.0 that was the KV runway and the start could fail; in 1.7.1 it is the split's set-up, so the start succeeds and serves on the GPU alone. 1.7.0's split, when its start succeeded there, halved the first token at 512 to 2,016 tokens.
- **A Neural Engine calibration taken while another app uses the Neural Engine is remembered**, and the split then serves a smaller share than a quiet calibration would choose, still faster than the GPU alone. Start once with `--recalibrate-ane`, or with Recalibrate Neural Engine on in the app, to calibrate again.
- **If the Neural Engine's system service stops answering**, each start waits 210 s before it serves on the GPU alone.
